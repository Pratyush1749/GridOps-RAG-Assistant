Substation Operations Overview

A substation is a facility that transforms voltage between transmission and
distribution levels, or between two distribution voltage classes, and provides
switching points where feeders, transformers, and protective equipment connect
to the wider grid. Substations are classified by function as transmission
substations (typically 115 kV and above), distribution substations (typically
4 kV to 34.5 kV on the low side), or switching substations, which route power
between circuits without changing voltage.

Core equipment in a typical substation includes:

- Power transformers, which step voltage up or down between the transmission
  and distribution systems.
- Circuit breakers and reclosers, which interrupt fault current and can
  automatically re-energize a feeder after a transient fault clears.
- Bus bars, which distribute incoming power to multiple outgoing feeders.
- Protective relays, which monitor current, voltage, and frequency and trip
  breakers when they detect a fault condition.
- SCADA remote terminal units (RTUs), which report equipment status and
  alarms back to the control center and accept remote switching commands.

Substation status is tracked as in_service (normal operation), maintenance
(equipment de-energized for planned work, typically under a clearance/tagout
procedure), or de-energized (unplanned outage or emergency shutdown).

Routine operations at a substation include switching operations (opening or
closing breakers/disconnects to isolate equipment or restore service),
periodic inspection of transformer oil levels and temperatures, relay testing,
and vegetation management around the perimeter. Any switching operation that
changes the state of equipment feeding customers must be logged with a time,
operator name, and reason, and coordinated with the control center before
execution unless it is an emergency response to an active fault.

Access to a substation requires authorization; unaccompanied entry by
personnel without substation entry training is not permitted, and all
switching must follow the utility's approved switching order format,
verified by a second qualified person before execution on transmission-class
equipment.
