Grid Reliability Metrics Overview

Utilities track a standard set of reliability indices to measure how well
the distribution grid serves customers, and to report performance to
regulators. These metrics are calculated from outage records (severity,
customers_affected, started_at, restored_at) rolled up across a reporting
period, typically monthly and annually.

Key indices:

- SAIDI (System Average Interruption Duration Index): the average number of
  minutes a customer was without power during the period, calculated as the
  sum of (customers_affected x outage duration in minutes) across all
  outages, divided by the total number of customers served. SAIDI is the
  most commonly cited "headline" reliability number.
- SAIFI (System Average Interruption Frequency Index): the average number of
  sustained interruptions a customer experienced during the period,
  calculated as the sum of customers_affected across all sustained outages,
  divided by the total number of customers served.
- CAIDI (Customer Average Interruption Duration Index): the average
  restoration time per interrupted customer, equal to SAIDI divided by
  SAIFI. CAIDI answers "when a customer's power does go out, how long does
  it typically take to come back."
- MAIFI (Momentary Average Interruption Frequency Index): the average number
  of momentary interruptions (successful automatic reclose events, typically
  under 5 minutes) a customer experienced. MAIFI is tracked separately from
  SAIDI/SAIFI because momentary interruptions do not count as sustained
  outages in most regulatory frameworks, even though they are noticeable to
  customers (clocks resetting, equipment rebooting).

Most regulators exclude "major event days" from standard SAIDI/SAIFI
reporting, typically defined statistically as a day where daily SAIDI
exceeds a threshold based on historical daily SAIDI variability. This
prevents a single severe storm from making a utility's routine, non-storm
reliability performance look artificially poor, while major event days are
still reported separately.

Utilities use these indices at multiple levels of granularity: system-wide
(for regulatory reporting), by region, and by individual feeder, to
prioritize capital investment. A feeder with SAIDI or SAIFI significantly
above the system average across multiple years is typically flagged as a
"worst-performing feeder" and becomes a candidate for targeted
reliability-improvement work such as additional sectionalizing switches,
tree-trimming cycles shortened from the standard rotation, or conversion
from overhead to underground construction in high-outage segments.
