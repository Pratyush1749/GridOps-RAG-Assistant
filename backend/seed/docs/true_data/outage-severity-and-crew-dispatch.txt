Outage Severity Classification and Crew Dispatch

Every outage recorded in the operations database is assigned a severity of
P1 through P4 at the time it is opened. Severity drives both dispatch
priority and the reliability targets the utility is measured against.

Severity definitions:

- P1: an outage affecting a critical facility (hospital, water treatment
  plant, emergency services), a large customer count (typically over 1,000
  meters on a single feeder), or presenting a public safety hazard such as a
  downed conductor. Target MTTR: under 2 hours. P1 outages require immediate
  crew dispatch and, for events over a defined customer-count threshold,
  notification to the regulatory/compliance team.
- P2: a significant but non-critical outage, typically several hundred
  customers on a feeder segment. Target MTTR: under 4 hours.
- P3: a localized outage, often a single transformer or a short feeder
  section, affecting a small number of customers. Target MTTR: under 8 hours.
- P4: a single-customer or minor outage (for example, a single service drop
  fault) with no broader feeder impact. Target MTTR: under 24 hours, and
  frequently handled during standard business hours rather than after-hours
  dispatch.

Crew dispatch process:

1. When an outage is opened, the dispatch system identifies the nearest
   available crew based on current location and whether they are already
   assigned to another job.
2. The crew is dispatched with the outage location, known fault indicators
   (if any), and severity. Dispatch time and crew name are logged in the
   crew dispatch log.
3. Response time is measured from dispatch to on-site arrival. Utilities
   typically target a P1 response time under 45 minutes in normal weather
   conditions; response-time targets are relaxed during declared storm
   events, when multiple simultaneous outages compete for a limited number
   of crews.
4. During major storm events, dispatch shifts from a strict severity-first
   model to a "greatest number of customers restored per crew-hour" model,
   which can mean a large P2 outage is worked before a single-customer P1
   safety hazard is fully resolved, provided the safety hazard has been
   made safe (for example, the area has been cordoned off) in the interim.
5. After restoration, the crew records the root cause, which is summarized
   in the outage's rca_summary field, and the outage record is closed with a
   restored_at timestamp used to compute MTTR.

MTTR (mean time to restore) is the primary internal reliability metric,
reported per severity tier, per region, and per feeder, and is a leading
indicator utilities monitor to identify feeders that need capital
investment (undergrounding, additional sectionalizing, or vegetation
clearance) to reduce repeat outages.
