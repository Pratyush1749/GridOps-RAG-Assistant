Feeder Outage Restoration Procedure

A feeder is a distribution circuit that carries power from a substation to a
group of transformers and, ultimately, to customer meters. When a feeder
experiences a fault (for example vegetation contact, equipment failure, or
storm damage), protective relays at the substation trip the feeder breaker,
de-energizing every meter downstream until the fault is cleared and the
feeder is restored.

Standard restoration sequence:

1. Confirm the outage. SCADA will report the feeder breaker as open and may
   raise a scada_alarm of type "breaker trip". Cross-check against customer
   outage calls and smart-meter "last gasp" signals to estimate the number of
   customers affected.
2. Attempt automatic reclose. Many feeder breakers are configured to
   automatically attempt one or more recloses within seconds of a trip, since
   a large share of faults (for example a tree branch making momentary
   contact) are transient and clear themselves. If reclose succeeds, the
   feeder re-energizes automatically and the event is logged as a momentary
   interruption.
3. If reclose fails, the outage is treated as sustained and a crew is
   dispatched. Dispatch priority follows outage severity: P1 (large-scale,
   critical facilities, or safety hazard), P2 (significant customer count),
   P3 (localized, non-critical), P4 (single customer or minor).
4. The crew patrols the feeder from the substation outward, or from known
   fault-indicator locations, to locate the fault. Fault indicators mounted
   on the line will flash or drop a target near the fault location, which
   substantially reduces patrol time compared to a full-line walk.
5. Once located, the crew isolates the faulted section by opening sectionalizing
   switches on either side of the damage, allowing the unaffected portion of
   the feeder to be re-energized from the substation (or, where a tie switch
   exists, from a neighboring feeder) while repairs proceed on the isolated
   section.
6. After repair, the crew closes switches to restore the isolated section and
   confirms normal voltage and load before closing out the job.

Restoration time (MTTR) is measured from the outage start time to the
restored_at timestamp and is a key reliability metric. Utilities typically
target a P1 MTTR under two hours and use momentary-vs-sustained
classification, along with SAIDI/SAIFI reliability indices, to report
performance to regulators.
