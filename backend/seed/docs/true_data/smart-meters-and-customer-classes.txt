Smart Meters and Customer Classes

Every point where the distribution grid connects to a customer is
represented by a meter, attached to the feeder that serves it. Meters are
categorized by customer_type: residential, commercial, or industrial.
Residential meters make up the large majority of the meter population but
each carries relatively small load; industrial meters are far fewer in
number but each can represent a significant share of a feeder's total
capacity, so a single industrial meter's status is often monitored more
closely than an individual residential meter.

Modern deployments use AMI (Advanced Metering Infrastructure) smart meters,
which report interval consumption data and can send a "last gasp" message
over their communication network at the moment power is lost, before their
backup capacitor depletes. Last-gasp signals from a cluster of meters on the
same feeder arriving within a short window are one of the fastest ways to
confirm and scope an outage, often faster than waiting for customer calls.

Meter status values:

- active: normal service, meter is communicating and billing normally.
- inactive: the account is not currently billed to an occupant (for example,
  a vacant property), but the meter remains physically connected and
  energized.
- disconnected: service has been physically or remotely disconnected, either
  for non-payment (following the utility's disconnection policy and
  required notice periods) or at the customer's request.

When evaluating the customer impact of a feeder outage, only meters with
status active or inactive on that feeder should be counted toward
customers_affected; disconnected meters were already without service prior
to the outage and should not be double-counted in reliability reporting.

Restoring a feeder does not guarantee every downstream meter regains power
individually. Some interruptions occur only on a single service lateral (for
example, a fault in the wire between a distribution transformer and one
customer) rather than the whole feeder, and these single-meter events are
tracked and dispatched the same way as any other outage, typically
classified P4 given the limited customer count.
