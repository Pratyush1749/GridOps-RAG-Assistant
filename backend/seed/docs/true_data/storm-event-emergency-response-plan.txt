Storm Event Emergency Response Plan

When weather forecasting indicates a high probability of widespread outages
(major windstorm, ice storm, or hurricane), the utility activates its
Emergency Response Plan (ERP) ahead of the event rather than waiting for
outages to occur.

Pre-storm activation steps:

1. The control center is staffed to 24-hour coverage and additional
   dispatchers are brought on to handle an expected surge in simultaneous
   outage events.
2. Internal crews are placed on standby, and if the forecast severity
   exceeds internal crew capacity, mutual-aid crews are requested from
   neighboring utilities under existing mutual-assistance agreements.
3. Staging sites are identified near the expected impact area so crews and
   material (poles, transformers, wire, crossarms) can be pre-positioned
   rather than dispatched from a central depot after the storm hits.
4. Substations and feeders serving critical facilities (hospitals, water
   treatment, emergency dispatch centers) are reviewed for backup supply
   options (alternate feed, mobile generation) in case primary supply is
   lost.

During-storm operations differ from blue-sky (normal weather) operations in
several ways:

- Dispatch priority shifts from strict severity order to a
  "restore-the-most-customers-per-crew-hour" model, described in the outage
  severity and crew dispatch procedure, since crew capacity is the binding
  constraint during a major event.
- Individual outage tickets may be consolidated: a single major storm can
  generate thousands of outage events in a short window, and dispatch groups
  nearby events into a single restoration job where a common cause (for
  example, a fallen tree taking down multiple spans) is identified.
- Public communication shifts to broader estimated-restoration-time (ERT)
  updates by region rather than per-customer updates, since individual ERTs
  cannot be reliably estimated until damage assessment is complete.
- A storm is typically declared a "major event day" for reliability
  reporting purposes if daily system SAIDI exceeds the statistical threshold
  described in the grid reliability metrics overview; outages during a major
  event day are excluded from routine (blue-sky) SAIDI/SAIFI reporting but
  are still tracked and reported separately to regulators.

After the event, the utility conducts a storm debrief covering total
customers affected, restoration timeline against the ERP's targets, mutual-aid
crew performance, and any equipment or process gaps identified, feeding into
updates to the Emergency Response Plan for the next event.
